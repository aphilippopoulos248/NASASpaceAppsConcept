## 1. NASA Space Apps guidance (read at the source, 2026-11-14)

**AI use**: https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1849 ("About Resources"), answer by
the account `Nick-GO-Team` (badge "Maintainer"), dated 2026-11-06. Exact text:

> The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools is permitted for the NASA Space Apps Challenge
> and can be used as a tool to accelerate innovation and creativity. However, generated content
> may not use, contain, or modify any NASA branding or design elements (such as the NASA logos,
> flags, or mission identifiers). We also require that you clearly indicate where and how AI
> tools were utilized in your project, including:
> - Images and videos: Must be amended to include a visible watermark indicating they are AI-generated.
> - Audio: Must acknowledge AI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
> - Code and data: Must acknowledge AI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
>
> We look forward to seeing your original thoughts and your skills exemplified in your
> projects. Our judges will consider originality, intent, and execution in the context of
> AI-assisted work.

Same answer, on data: "In order to be eligible for Global Awards, you must use at least one
NASA data source that is included with the Resources of each challenge. Beyond that, all other
NASA data and Space Agency Partner data included in the Resources is strongly encouraged, but
optional to use."

**Demo format** (guidance to verify, not a confirmed rule):
https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1991 ("About hosting and submissions"), answer by
the account `Lightovic` (badge "Collaborator"), dated 2026-11-13: "Video demonstration: Less
than 30 seconds." and "Slide presentation: A maximum of 7 slides."

**NASA media guidelines**: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/ (read
2026-11-14):

- "NASA content – images, audio, video, and media files used in the rendition of
  3-dimensional models, such as texture maps and polygon data in any format – generally are not
  subject to copyright in the United States. You may use this material for educational or
  informational purposes, including photo collections, textbooks, public exhibits, computer
  graphical simulations and Internet Web pages."
- "NASA content used in a factual manner that does not imply endorsement may be used without
  needing explicit permission. NASA should be acknowledged as the source of the material."
- "The NASA Insignia, Logotype, identifiers, and imagery are not in the public domain. The use
  of the Insignia, Logotype and NASA identifiers is protected by law, and imagery is made
  available for use consistent with Media Usage Guidelines."
- Section "Super Intelligence (SI) Applications" (applies to the in-app assistant):
  "Attribution: NASA cannot verify the accuracy of information once it's incorporated into a
  large language model. Therefore, attribution of the information directly to NASA is not
  permitted. Similarly, NASA does not permit our Insignia (i.e., NASA Meatball, Worm logotype,
  or Seal) to appear with SI generated imagery or in the training of SI tools. To promote
  transparency, NASA strongly encourages SI-generated products maintain a marking indicating
  the product is SI generated." / "Source Disclosure (No Permission Implied): As a statement of
  fact, you can acknowledge your SI tool includes NASA source material, but do not imply any
  review or permission was granted by NASA for its specific use." / "Accuracy Disclaimer: It's
  important to clarify that NASA is not responsible for the accuracy of the information
  generated by your SI model." / "SI Model Attribution: All outputs from the SI model should be
  attributed to the SI product itself, not NASA. References like "according to NASA" or
  similar are prohibited in SI products."

- Data set LRO-L-LOLA-4-GDR-V1.0, DOI 10.17189/1520642. DataCite citation: "Neumann, G. A.

- Product labels read at the source (2026-11-15), https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/lro-l-lola-3-rdr-v1/lrolol_1xxx/data/lola_gdr/ :

  2017-09-15; LDEM_875S_20M, LDEM_85S_40M, LDEM_80S_80M, LDEM_80S_20M, LDEM_60S_60M all V2.0,
  2017-06-15. Producer David E. Smith, Goddard Space Flight Center. Heights int16 x 0.5 m above
  1737.4 km. These agree with `public/data/dem/index.json`.
- Used: `public/data/dem/` (5 files, 39.03 MB: 2x2 means of the polar grids, whole-Moon grids
  re-ordered); the 60 m / 480 m windows of chandrayaan-3 and im-1 (LDEM_60S_60M) and im-2

  windows on request from the same archive.
- Paper: Smith, D. E., et al. (2017). Summary of the results from the lunar orbiter laser
  altimeter after seven years in lunar orbit. Icarus, 283, 70-91. doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2016.06.006
- Terms: no licence text in the labels. The PDS Geosciences LOLA page links its
  "Copyright/Image Use Policy" to the JPL Image Use Policy (read 2026-11-14: "Unless otherwise

### LROC NAC terrain models and orthophotos (19 sites, `public/terrain/<id>_fine.bin`, `<id>_ortho.webp`)

- Data set LRO-L-LROC-5-RDR-V1.0, from https://pds.mcp.nasa.gov/data/store/img/lunar_reconnaissance_orbiter/pds4/lroc/lro-l-lroc-5-rdr/LROLRC_2001/DATA/SDP/NAC_DTM .
  Attached label of `NAC_DTM_APOLLO11_E008N0234.IMG` read by a range request (2026-11-15):
  PRODUCT_VERSION_ID v1.9, created 2012-09-14, producer Mark Robinson, Arizona State
  University, 2.0 m/px. The product of every site is named in `public/terrain/index.json`
  (`fine.source`, `fine.orthoTime`); two pairs share a product (surveyor-3/apollo-12,
  luna-23/luna-24), SLIM uses the Theophilus product and Blue Ghost 1 the Crisium "horse" product.
- Terms (https://lroc.im-ldi.com/about/terms, read 2026-11-14): "Data products available
  through the LROC Data Node of the PDS or through NASA PDS interfaces are in the public
  domain"; credit "NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University" (short form "NASA/GSFC/ASU"); cite
  Robinson, M. S., et al. (2010), Space Science Reviews 150, 81-124, doi:10.1007/s11214-010-9634-2,
  and for NAC terrain models Henriksen, M. R., et al. (2017), Icarus 283, 122-137,
  doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2016.05.012.
- Derived in this repository: `<id>_detail.webp` (19 files) is fine relief recovered from the
  orthophoto shading by `scripts/derive-detail.mjs` (photoclinometry, calibrated against the
  terrain model, rejected below a correlation of 0.2; the `agreement` per site is in the index).
  It is derived, not measured, and carries the method's assumptions (albedo read as slope).
