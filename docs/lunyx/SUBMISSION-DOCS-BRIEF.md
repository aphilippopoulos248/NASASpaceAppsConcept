# Submission documentation: what the operator requires

Working brief for task T-17 (owner @assistant). It collects the operator's requirements
from hub messages m221 and m273 so they do not have to fit into a task description.
The documents themselves are `README.md`, `docs/lunyx/SOURCES.md` and
`docs/lunyx/AI-USE.md`; `docs/lunyx/STATUS.md` (kept by the coordinator) is the factual
base for what is shipped, not shipped and validated.

## 1. Sources and licences (`docs/lunyx/SOURCES.md`)

Every entry is checked against the source itself, not copied from a handover note.

- Every dataset in `public/`: product name, URL, version or date, citation, licence or
  usage terms, what Lunyx did to it, and its size. At least: LOLA whole-Moon and polar
  grids and the site windows; SLDEM2015; LROC NAC elevation models and orthophotos; the
  global colour and normal maps (NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit); Blue Marble; Kaguya TC and LROC
  WAC tiles fetched at run time from NASA Moon Trek; the JPL Horizons tables; PGDA
  product 69; Lemelin et al. 2021 (CC BY 4.0); Diviner annual temperature maps
  (Schorghofer and Williams, Harvard Dataverse, CC0); the ShadowCam index.
- Provenance of the textures the teammate added on GitHub `main`
  (`public/textures/surface_detail_normal.jpg`, `surface_detail_tone.jpg`, built by
  `scripts/make-surface-detail.mjs`): establish from the script and the commit history
  whether they are generated by that script alone or derived from a third-party source,
  and say which.
- Dependency notices: every runtime and build dependency with its licence, read from the
  installed packages, including the two bundled font families.
- ShadowCam stays as credited links. No ShadowCam imagery is redistributed unless
  permission is established: its archive carries no licence or usage statement.
- Diviner is labelled by what the source provides: annual maximum and annual mean
  temperature. Not seasonal temperatures.

## 2. Scientific validation limits (README, SOURCES, STATUS)

- State what has been checked against what, with the tolerances, and what has not:
  `docs/lunyx/validation.md`, `tests/fixtures/horizons/ephemeris-check/` and
  `tests/fixtures/validation/ephemeris-check/` are the records.
- Do not describe the published build as fully validated. The first independent round
  (T-26) is complete with open follow-ups (T-31, T-32); a second round (T-34) is running.
- Quote findings as they stand, including the uncomfortable ones: builds up to `375f816`
  (the commit on GitHub `main`) read the skyline too low at crest sites; the app's
  "sunset" is the last limb while a published start of night matches the first cut of the
  Sun (decision D-8); the Chandrayaan-3 sunrise time is not confirmed by ISRO's date.
- The assistant has never been run against a real language model or the real Jev
  service on the build machine. Say so.

## 3. AI-use disclosure (`docs/lunyx/AI-USE.md`)

NASA's Global Organizing Team guidance, as relayed by the operator. Read it at the source
and quote it exactly rather than from this summary:
https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1849

- Disclose where and how AI was used.
- Visibly watermark AI-generated images and video.
- Acknowledge AI-generated code, audio and data in descriptive text and metadata.
- Do not generate or modify NASA branding.

What the document must contain:

- How AI was used to build Lunyx: code written by AI agents under human direction; the
  in-app assistant and the models it can call.
- An asset inventory: for every image, texture and data file, whether it is measured data,
  derived from measured data, procedurally generated by a script in this repository, or
  AI-generated. Verify each one; do not assume. A screenshot of AI-assisted software is not
  automatically an "AI-generated image".
- Whatever watermarking or metadata acknowledgement follows from that inventory, listed
  for the operator to approve before any asset is altered.
- A check that the app and the documents neither generate nor modify NASA branding (logos,
  insignia, wordmarks). Crediting NASA data in text is not branding.

## 4. Demo format

A Navigator advises a demo under 30 seconds or at most 7 slides:
https://github.com/nasa/spaceapps/discussions/1991. The official guide was not available
to the operator, so record this as guidance to verify, not as a confirmed rule.

## 5. Anything that cannot be established

A licence, a provenance or an AI status that cannot be established from the source goes
into a list for the operator. It is never filled with a guess.
